5 · New method: Reader-in-the-Loop PPO

Let the policy see what the reader found, and choose the final answer

DIAGNOSIS

Two leaks in plain PPO

  • Hop 2 is chosen blind: the policy never sees the span hop 1 produced, so it cannot follow a bridge entity
  • Max-confidence aggregation: the best span in the selected set gives 38.7 F1, the reader's pick only 27.6

FIX 1 · BRIDGE FEEDBACK

Reader output enters the state

  • After every hop the frozen reader is queried
  • 11 candidate features: does the bridge span appear in this row, title or passage; confidence; overlap with the question

FIX 2 · LEARNED COMMIT

One extra action after STOP

  • Picks which selected entity's answer is final
  • Logit = learned head + 5 × reader score, so at initialisation it equals max-confidence
  • Shaping generalised so it still telescopes

Also tested from the literature: Rao-Blackwellised COMMIT gradient, privileged critic, self-imitation, action dropout, EMA weights, RLOO / Dr. GRPO, test-time self-consistency

Our extension, reader-in-the-loop PPO. Diagnosing the PPO policy showed two leaks. First, it picks the second hop without knowing what the reader extracted from the first, so it cannot follow a bridge entity, which is the essence of multi-hop reasoning. Second, the final answer is chosen by the reader's maximum confidence across the selected entities: picking the best span in the selected set would give 38.7 F1, but max-confidence gives only 27.6, so about 11 points are lost purely at aggregation. Fix one, bridge feedback: after every hop we run the frozen reader and put its span into the observation, as features telling the policy whether the span appears in a candidate's text, title or row. Fix two, a learned COMMIT action: after STOP the policy chooses which of its selected entities supplies the answer. Its logit has a residual prior equal to five times the reader score, so an untrained head reproduces max-confidence and RL only learns the corrections. We generalised the potential-based shaping so it still telescopes with the extra step. We also implemented a set of ideas from the literature and tested each one: an exact Rao-Blackwellised gradient for the commit choice, an asymmetric privileged critic, self-imitation learning, action dropout, EMA weights, the critic-free optimisers RLOO and Dr. GRPO, and test-time self-consistency.
