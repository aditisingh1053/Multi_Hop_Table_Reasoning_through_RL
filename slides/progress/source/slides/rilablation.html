What helped and what did not. Left: the ablation. Bridge feedback alone adds 1.1, the learned COMMIT alone 1.4, and together 2.3 at 40 iterations, each significant, so both parts contribute and they roughly add up. Middle: the extensions from the literature, each added on top of RiL-PPO. Action dropout, from multi-hop knowledge-graph RL, is the only clear win at 31.1. The Rao-Blackwellised commit gradient is neutral on F1 but puts the answer in the evidence more often. EMA never got selected, so it is identical. The privileged critic and self-imitation both hurt by over three points: the privileged critic fits values better but the policy gets worse, and self-imitation replays early lucky episodes. Stacking everything is therefore worse than plain RiL-PPO, an honest negative result. Right: optimisers. RLOO matches PPO with GAE with no critic at all. Vanilla GRPO collapses to selecting one entity because its per-group standard-deviation normalisation amplifies noise under sparse rewards; Dr. GRPO, which removes it, recovers more than ten points. Finally, test-time self-consistency changed F1 by at most 0.2, so it is not worth the extra reader calls.
