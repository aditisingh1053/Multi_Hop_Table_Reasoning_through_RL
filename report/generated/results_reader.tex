Table~\ref{tab:reader} keeps every selector's evidence fixed and replaces the reader by RoBERTa-base, which was never used in training, on the first 1{,}000 dev questions. The ranking of the selectors does not change. PPO from scratch reaches \robScratch{} F1 with the new reader, against \robBmThree{} for BM25 top-3, \robReinforce{} for REINFORCE and \robBc{} for behaviour cloning, while the oracle reaches \robOracle{}; most selectors gain a little from the stronger reader, and PPO from scratch gains slightly more than the others (from \miniScratch{} to \robScratch{} on these questions). The learned selection is therefore not tied to the quirks of the reader it was trained with. So that the table is internally consistent, all of its second-reader entries were computed in one environment (the laptop CPU) from the stored selections; as a check, re-reading behaviour cloning's seed-0 selections there gave 16.0 EM / 20.4 F1, against 15.8 / 20.2 in the GPU environment.
